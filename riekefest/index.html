RiekeFest

The Growth of Infrared Astronomy and Active Galaxies from the Galactic Center to the Edge of the Universe

March 15–19, 2027  ·  Tucson, Arizona

RiekeFest will bring together researchers across infrared astronomy, galaxy evolution, and active galactic nuclei to celebrate the scientific contributions of George and Marcia Rieke and to discuss the discoveries their work has helped make possible.

Program Conference Information

About RiekeFest

For more than four decades, George and Marcia Rieke have been central figures in the development of infrared astronomy, from ground-based instrumentation to space missions including Spitzer and the James Webb Space Telescope. Their work has shaped how we study everything from the Solar System to the most distant galaxies, and has helped establish Steward Observatory and the University of Arizona as a world center for infrared astronomy.

RiekeFest is both a scientific conference and a celebration. It brings together colleagues, collaborators, and former students to survey the current state of infrared astronomy and the study of active galaxies, while honoring the scientific legacy that George and Marcia Rieke have built over their careers.

The meeting is organized around two connected themes: the growth of infrared astronomy as a discipline, and the galaxy activity and evolution science that infrared observations have made possible, from nearby active galactic nuclei to the earliest galaxies in the Universe.

Scientific Program

The scientific program spans five days and two connected themes. Detailed session times, speakers, and chairs will be added below as they are confirmed.

March 15–16, 2027

The Growth of Infrared Astronomy

These two days trace the development of infrared astronomy and its impact across astrophysics, highlighting the instrumentation and science that George and Marcia Rieke helped pioneer.

  • Instrumentation & the Development of Infrared Astronomy

  • Solar System & Exoplanets

  • Stellar & Galactic Astronomy

  • Galaxy Evolution

  • Early Universe & Cosmology

  • Legacy of Major Infrared Facilities

March 17–19, 2027

Active Galaxies and Galaxy Evolution

The remaining three days focus on galaxy activity and evolution, including the growth of supermassive black holes and the discoveries made possible by Spitzer and JWST.

  • Supermassive Black Hole Growth

  • AGN & Galaxy Co-evolution

  • Dust & Obscured Activity

  • Star Formation & Galaxy Evolution

  • Early Galaxies & Cosmic Evolution

  • Spitzer & JWST Discoveries
